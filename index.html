About

I'm an AI engineer and full-stack developer. Most of what I build sits in one place: the layer between a model's output and someone relying on it. That means agent loops with governed tool use, plain deterministic code on the paths where a model error would be dangerous, and evaluation harnesses that say whether the thing actually works.

Since 2025 I've built and published several systems that way: a parametric building engine, a voice-driven retrieval companion with a custom agent loop, an on-device health-intake tool, orchestration telemetry over live agent sessions, and a text-analysis research system whose measuring instruments are checked before they're trusted. Two are open source; the others are described below.

I took a career break from 2022 to 2025 and have been building continuously since.

From 2011 to 2022 I built and ran commercial configurator and custom CPQ systems for configurable physical products (sheds, playsets, fencing). The shed configurator began in 2012; in 2015–2016 I acquired the business and ran it independently as 3D Customizer, in production through 2022. It processed 10,000+ quote, order, and rent-to-own transactions tied to tens of millions of dollars in shed sales. Before that I was the technical-to-business contact across 40 to 60 agency client accounts, from small businesses to multimillion-dollar companies, turning requirements into feasible tools.

My hands-on background in construction, sales, warehouse management, and B2B marketing gives me practical context for building software around real products, customers, and operations.

How I build

I design the system and its invariants. AI agents write the code, across several vendors. Nothing lands without independent re-verification, mutation-tested tests, and a receipt. Agent output is verified before it is trusted.

Agent systems

Agentic Retrieval Companion

A voice agent over a live world model: a custom, provider-agnostic agent loop (LLM to tool dispatch and back, with a round budget), an MCP server exposing nine tools, and a truth contract where unexplored is reported as unknown rather than empty. The router was evaluated on 700 questions across three runs, came back at about 5% exact match, and was replaced. Hundreds of tests. The world model is fed by game telemetry.
